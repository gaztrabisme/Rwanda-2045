# CLAUDE.md — Kigarama Ridge (working title)

Worldbuilding bible for a slice-of-life story set in Rwanda, 2045. Read this before writing or extending anything in this world.

---

## 1. Premise

Rwanda, 2045. In Bugesera, south of Kigali, the government and a farmers' cooperative built a large terraced apartment complex for agricultural workers. Every unit has a garden balcony. Around the towers sit shared parks, herb gardens, greenhouses, fish ponds and open cooperative fields run with heavy automation.

Living here is a status symbol. Farming has become a high-class profession. The complex produces as much as a whole province.

**Why Rwanda:** densest country in mainland Africa, hills already terraced, land is the scarcest resource. Stacking homes vertically frees the ground for fields.

**Why Bugesera:** flat, dry, historically drought-prone, near the large new airport. A thriving farm community here is a national showpiece.

---

## 2. Tone and storytelling rules

- **Genre:** slice of life. Mundane interpersonal drama. No villains, no thrillers.
- **Implicit worldbuilding.** Never explain a system. Show someone using it, complaining about it, or gossiping about it.
- **Technology is boring to the characters.** Drones are like buses: late, noisy, taken for granted.
- **Status shows through small objects:** whose balcony has rare plants, who buys bread outside instead of from the co-op kitchen.
- **The outside world appears at the edges:** relatives from Kigali, smallholders at the market gate, foreign buyers.
- **Big-picture threads (economics, social change, geopolitics) stay in the background.** One carrier (a person or object) per thread. At most one line per thread per scene. Characters care only about the personal side.
- **Prose stays accessible.** Density comes through image, metaphor and dialogue, not terminology. Avoid jargon.

---

## 3. The complex

**Layout (inside → out):**
apartments → shared parks and herb gardens → greenhouses and fish ponds → open cooperative fields → market gate facing the highway

- Terraced towers step down a slope, echoing Rwanda's hillside terraces.
- Each unit has a deep garden balcony, privately owned produce.
- Closed loops: kitchen waste → biogas and compost; fish water → greenhouses; roofs → rainwater storage for dry seasons.

**Seasons (real Rwandan calendar):** long rains, long dry, short rains, short dry. Use these to structure time.

## 4. Work and roles

| Role | Work |
|---|---|
| Field crews | Supervise drones, robot weeders, irrigation. Few people, big acreage |
| Garden keepers | Run shared herb and vegetable plots, train kids |
| Kitchen guild | Process crops into dried spices, juices, flour, sauces. Higher value than raw produce. Also caters events |
| Traders | Sell outside: Kigali markets, air-export of herbs (Gulf, Europe), hotel contracts |
| Repair guild | Keep machines running. Quietly powerful, always on call |
| Seed and soil lab | Seed bank and soil testing. Elders plus young scientists |
| Weather and data desk | Forecasts, planting plans; sells data to outside farms |
| Cold chain and transport | Trucks, drone delivery, storage |

## 5. Economy and institutions

- **Cooperative shares:** each household owns shares. Field profits split by shares plus hours worked.
- **Balcony gardens are personal.** Owners sell through the co-op or privately. Selling privately is frowned on.
- **Waiting list** to move in. City professionals want in; some try to buy or fake their way on.
- **Umuganda** (real: Rwanda's monthly national community work day) → here, a monthly all-hands harvest or repair day.
- **Imihigo** (real: public performance pledges signed by local leaders) → the co-op signs yearly yield targets with the government, made public. Missing them is a public embarrassment.
- Shares have grown in value for years. Older people remember when a share "wasn't worth a goat."

## 7. Current story: the wedding

**Couple:** Aline (trader) and Jean-Paul (field crew).
Her family thinks she's marrying down; his family thinks she's getting above herself. Neither says it.

**Real customs used:**
- **Gusaba** — the "asking" ceremony. Each family has a spokesperson; teasing, poetic speeches; part theatre, part negotiation.
- **Inkwano** — bride price, traditionally cows. Here: a cow from the co-op dairy herd plus cooperative shares.
- Then church or civil wedding, then reception with dancers.

**Beats:**
1. Wedding lands on the monthly work day; the co-op board is slow to grant an exemption.
2. Jean-Paul's family picks Mzee Gatera as gusaba spokesperson; everyone fears the three stories.
3. Aline's mother orders flowers from outside; Claudine hears about it in the lift.
4. The fathers argue whether the shares come from Jean-Paul's household or his whole family — a fight about who owns what.
5. Kitchen guild caters; Aline's trader colleagues suggest a better supplier.
6. Eric is called away mid-reception for a broken irrigation pump.
7. Afterwards: the new family's kid is caught trading wedding sweets for seedlings.

**Undercurrents:** trader money vs grower status; old ways of marrying vs new ways of owning; the floor as a family that doesn't always like itself.

## 8. Background threads (carriers at the wedding)

| Thread | Carrier |
|---|---|
| Rising share value | Fathers' bride-price argument; old uncle's goat line |
| Regional food prices | Trader colleagues talking shop; Dubai herb prices; someone checks a phone and goes quiet |
| Smallholders losing out | Jean-Paul's cousin farms outside the walls, brings a gift of his own beans |
| City/country flip | Overdressed Kigali aunt calls it "the village" until she sees the balconies |
| Pan-African free trade | Ghanaian wedding cloth, Kenyan speakers — unremarked, normal |
| Foreign seed licences | Seed company guest invited via Aline; growers are polite and nothing more |
| Climate pressure | Late rains; every toast mentions rain; elders watch the sky |
| Regional migration | Reception staff with outside accents who live beyond the complex; nobody asks |
| Women's earning power | Aline paid for part of the reception; her mother hides it |
| Automation and jobs | Harvester vote next week (would cut half the field crews); crews go quiet near board members |

**Collision idea:** seed company guest seated next to the cousin with the beans. Nothing happens.
**Payoff idea:** rain starts during the last dance; old men stop dancing to look at it.
**Epilogue idea:** months later, told through one object — the wedding cloth covering seedlings, or a harvester-vote notice pinned in the lift.
